Buying tips by part

CPU

Match to your task: gaming favours high clocks and 6–8 strong cores; creation, programming and streaming benefit from more cores. Make sure the socket matches your motherboard.

GPU

Buy the most GPU your budget allows for gaming and creation. Check it physically fits your case length, and that VRAM suits your resolution.

RAM

16GB is the floor; 32GB is the sweet spot for creation, programming and streaming. Match DDR4/DDR5 to the board and buy two sticks for dual-channel.

Storage

A 1TB+ NVMe SSD as the main drive is the biggest felt speed-up. Add a big HDD only for cheap bulk storage.

PSU

Size it above your total draw with headroom for future upgrades, and prefer an 80+ Gold or better unit from a reputable brand.

Cooler

Match cooling to the CPU's TDP. A big air tower or a 240mm+ AIO for hot chips; check air-cooler height fits the case.

Glossary

CPU
The processor — runs the operating system, games logic, compilers. More cores help multitasking, rendering and compiling; high clock speed helps single-threaded work and game frame rates.
GPU
The graphics card — renders games and accelerates video/AI work. The single biggest factor in gaming frame rates.
VRAM
Memory on the GPU. Higher resolutions, texture detail and creative workloads need more; 8GB is fine for 1080p, 16GB+ suits 4K and content creation.
TDP
Thermal Design Power (watts) — roughly how much heat a part makes and power it draws. Used to size the cooler and PSU.
Socket
The physical CPU-to-motherboard connector (e.g. AM5, LGA1700). The CPU and motherboard sockets must match.
Form factor
Board/case size class — ATX (large), mATX (medium), ITX (small). The case must support the motherboard's form factor.
DDR4 / DDR5
RAM generations. A motherboard supports one or the other, not both — match your RAM to the board.
NVMe SSD
The fastest common storage, plugged straight into the motherboard. Much quicker than SATA SSDs and far quicker than hard drives (HDDs).
PSU
Power supply. Should comfortably exceed the system's total draw — aim for ~30% headroom. 80+ Gold/Platinum ratings mean better efficiency.
AIO
All-in-one liquid cooler. Larger radiators (240/360mm) dissipate more heat and suit high-TDP CPUs; big air coolers are a quieter, simpler alternative.
